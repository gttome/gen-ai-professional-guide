Tone Changer Prompt

USE CASES

The following examples demonstrate the "Tone Changer" methodology applied to professional scenarios where the emotional delivery of a message is as important as the content itself.

CUSTOMER SERVICE DE-ESCALATION


Scenario:
A Support Agent has written a frustrated draft response to a rude customer and needs to convert it into a professional, de-escalating reply without losing the core facts.

Prompt:
[Standard Syntax]
Input Text: "You can't just demand a refund because you broke the screen. We told you in the manual that drops aren't covered. Read the warranty."
Target Tone: 2. Apologetic (or 15. Objective)
(Goal: To soften the blow while maintaining policy firmness.)



SALES & MARKETING COPYWRITING


Scenario:
A Product Marketer has a dry technical specification sheet for a new coffee machine and wants to turn it into an exciting social media launch post.

Prompt:
[Standard Syntax]
Input Text: "The Model X features a dual-boiler system and maintains water temperature at 93 degrees Celsius. It has a 2-liter tank and fits on standard counters."
Target Tone: 6. Enthusiastic (or 17. Persuasive)
(Goal: To transform specs into benefits and excitement.)



EXECUTIVE LEADERSHIP COMMUNIQUÉ


Scenario:
A CEO needs to address the company regarding a missed quarterly target. The initial draft sounds too negative, and they want to pivot to a message of resilience and future opportunity.

Prompt:
[Standard Syntax]
Input Text: "We failed to hit our Q3 numbers. The market is bad and sales were slow. We need to work harder or Q4 will also be a disaster."
Target Tone: 11. Inspirational (or 16. Optimistic)
(Goal: To acknowledge the failure without demoralizing the workforce.)



LEGAL & COMPLIANCE DOCUMENTATION


Scenario:
A HR Manager has rough notes from a disciplinary meeting that are too casual and conversational. They need to formalize them for the official employee file.

Prompt:
[Standard Syntax]
Input Text: "So I sat down with John and told him he's gotta stop coming in late. He said traffic is bad, but I said that's not an excuse. He promised to try better."
Target Tone: 7. Formal (or 15. Objective)
(Goal: To create a legally defensible record of the conversation.)



INTERNAL TEAM FEEDBACK


Scenario:
A Team Lead wants to give feedback on a project that is technically correct but missed the deadline. They want to be firm about the deadline without damaging the relationship.

Prompt:
[Standard Syntax]
Input Text: "Great job on the code, but you were late again. This messes up the release schedule. Don't let it happen next time."
Target Tone: 3. Assertive (or 8. Friendly)
(Goal: To correct behavior clearly while maintaining team morale.)
